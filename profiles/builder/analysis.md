# Evaluation goal

Score how useful this item is to one specific reader: a computer-engineering student who wants to earn well as a programmer, through freelancing, remote work, building a product, or a high-paying backend/AI/infrastructure job. They want to learn only what pays. Judge usefulness to this reader, not general importance to the tech industry.

# What this reader cares about, in priority order

1. **Backend, infra and AI tools they can use.** New or significantly updated APIs, frameworks, databases, cloud services, AI models and agent tooling; meaningful pricing or capability changes; practical engineering deep-dives on building, scaling or running backend systems, data pipelines and automation.
2. **Ways to make money with code.** Product launches and Show HN projects in automation, bots, developer tools, AI tools or SaaS; indie or bootstrapped business stories with concrete numbers; new platforms or APIs that open a freelance or product opportunity; payments, platform or policy changes that affect people selling software.
3. **Jobs and the freelance market.** Hiring and layoff trends, salaries and pay data, remote work, skills employers are paying for, interview and career discussions with substance, freelancing rates and client experiences.
4. **Major tech and AI headlines.** Big launches, model releases and industry moves that any working developer is expected to know about.

# Push down

- **Security incidents** (CVEs, breaches, exploits): score 3-5 unless the issue sits in widely used backend tooling the reader would realistically deploy (for example a popular web framework, database, language runtime or cloud service) and requires action. Then score on usefulness of the action, not on severity.
- **Hardware, chips, devices and academic research papers**: score 2-4 unless there is an immediate, practical effect on software developers' tools, costs or jobs.
- **Politics, government and general news**: score 2-4 unless it directly changes AI rules, tech hiring, remote work, or software business.
- Nostalgia, retro computing, trivia, opinion essays with no practical takeaway: 2-4.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Act on this.** A tool, change or opportunity this reader should look at this week, or a major development that changes what skills pay.
- **7-8: Worth reading today.** Clearly useful for their skills, projects, income or job search.
- **5-6: Nice to know.** Relevant but marginal or incremental.
- **3-4: Low value for this reader.** Important to someone else, or too far from their goals.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, promotion without substance, off-topic.

# Evaluation guidance

Prefer concrete details (names, versions, prices, numbers, availability) over hype. Popularity is weak evidence: a heavily upvoted story can still be irrelevant to this reader. Do not reward exaggerated headlines or vendor claims without substance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
